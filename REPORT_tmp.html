Building the GuaranIA LLM

This is a living document that describes the approach followed to develop the LLMs for the project GuaranIA. The building process follows the BYOL methodology, which is implemented through the in-house framework Guaratune. The main idea is to adapt a multilingual open-source base model (e.g., Goolge Gemma-4) to Guarani while aiming to retain its existing capabilities.

BYOL methodology in a nutshell

The BYOL methodology consists of three steps: continual pretraining (CPT), where the base model continues training on a curated langage corpus, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), where the CPT model is futher trained on instruction-and-response examples, and model merging, where the adapted model is combined with the original model in weight space. See the BYOL paper for more information about the framework.

GuaranIA approach

Continual pretraining (CPT)

Finding the dataset configuration

The guarani corpus Kuatia augmented with Spanish and English FineWeb-Edu samples are proposed to be used to build the GuaranIA LLMs. The three sources were combined into eigth different data configurations for full CPT (i.e., all model weights are updated) of Gemma-4 E4B-PT to identify the most promising configuration.

  • C1: Kuatia (43,677,319 tokens)
  • C2: Kuatia without synthetic documents (27,994,685 tokens)
  • C3: Kuatia + 20% Spanish FineWeb-Edu (52,415,631 tokens)
  • C4: Kuatia without synthetic documents + 20% Spanish FineWeb-Edu (36,732,997 tokens)
  • C5: Kuatia + 20% FineWeb-Edu (52,412,827 tokens)
  • C6: Kuatia without synthetic documents + 20% FineWeb-Edu (36,730,193 tokens)
  • C7: Kuatia + 10% Spanish FineWeb-Edu + 10% FineWeb-Edu (52,415,521 tokens)
  • C8: Kuatia without synthetic documents + 10% Spanish FineWeb-Edu + 10% FineWeb-Edu (36,732,887 tokens)
CPT Hyperparameters

For Gemma-4 E4B CPT, the experiment configuration uses a sequence length of 2,048, four epochs, a per-device batch size of 1, and a learning rate of 2e-5 with a cosine scheduler and 2e-6 minimum. Training uses packed sequences, bfloat16, AdamW (betas 0.9 and 0.999), 3% warmup, and gradient clipping at 1.0. Evaluation runs at the end of each epoch. Full CPT uses gradient accumulation of 32 and weight decay of 0.01.

Deep-dive into the guarani mixed performance

The adapted model's mixed performance on the Guarani benchmarks may have three explanations. First, the base model's existing Guarani capabilities may limit the gains CPT can deliver on these tasks. Second, the CPT data may not provide enough additional information to substantially improve the model's task performance. Third, the size of the adapted model is too large compared with the volume of data, producing less promising gains.

To further investigate the first two explainations, we run additional experiments with a previous generation of the Gemma base model (Gemma-3 4B), which passes through a full CPT process using the C7 data configuration. The results of these experiments can use to compare the performance of base models and also to look at the performance gains. If the performance of the Gemma-3 base is noticeable inferior than the performance of the Gemma-4 base and the gains of the adapted Gemma-3 are larger than the adapted Gemma-4 we can confirm the superior capacity of the latter to solve the guarani tasks. On the other hand, if the performance and gains are similar, the CPT data do not add much more benefits to improve the model's performance.

The table below shows that Gemma-4 E4B starts from a higher Guarani average than Gemma-3 4B (31.66 vs. 22.37) and also reaches a higher average after C7 CPT (37.42 vs. 33.18). However, the average gain is larger for Gemma-3 4B (+10.81 vs. +5.76). This pattern is consistent with the idea that the stronger base model has less room to improve through CPT, while the lower-scoring base model has more headroom. Compared with Gemma-4 E4B, Gemma-3 4B gains more from CPT on almost every tasks, except in WNLI. In the case of translation we see mixed results. Gemma-4 E4B gains more on translation into Guarani, while Gemma-3 4B shows larger gains for translation from Guarani. From these results, it seems that, so far, what might explain the mixed performance of the adapted Gemma-4 is the superior capacity of its baseline, not the trained data content.

The smaller Gemma-4 E2B gains more on average from C7 than Gemma-4 E4B (+8.23 vs. +5.76 points). E2B also gains more on Guarani Global MMLU Lite, WNLI, MultiWikiQA, and translation from Guarani; E4B gains more on Belebele and translation into Guarani. Neither model improves on MGSM. This pattern is consistent with the C7 corpus providing a better data-to-model-size balance for E2B, but it is not conclusive: E4B starts from a higher average and still finishes with the higher score (37.42 vs. 31.26), while the relative gains vary by task. The results support the model-size hypothesis as one possible explanation for the mixed performance, rather than showing that model size alone causes it.

Multilingual performance

Across Spanish and English benchmarks, the CPT variants sometimes score above the base and sometimes below it, as the tables below depict. Although the average scores are generally lower, the gaps from the base are modest. The smallest Spanish gap is 2.83 points (C8=62.82 vs. base=65.65), and the smallest English gap is 2.20 points (C5=63.15 vs. base=65.35). This overall proximity suggests that multilingual performance is retained through CPT, despite task-level gains and losses.

Data configuration finding

C7 shows to be the most promising data configuration to carry forward to supervised fine-tuning. It has the best perplexity results, leads translation from Guarani, scores highest on Guarani Belebele, and comes closest to the base on Guarani Global MMLU Lite and MGSM. It also retains multilingual performance well: its English average ranks second among CPT variants, and its Spanish average is among the strongest.

[!IMPORTANT] While no single configuration leads every evaluation, the dataset configuration C7 shows to offer the strongest balance across the Guarani capabilities and multilingual retention.
